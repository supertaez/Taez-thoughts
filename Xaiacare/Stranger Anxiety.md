## Definition and Overview  
**Stranger anxiety** is a developmental phenomenon observed in infants and toddlers, characterized by distress or wariness in the presence of unfamiliar individuals. It typically emerges around **6–12 months of age** and diminishes as children develop [[Social Referencing]] and broader social skills.  

> [!NOTE]- Key Insight  
> Stranger anxiety is **not pathological**—it reflects normative cognitive growth, signaling the infant’s ability to distinguish familiar caregivers from strangers ([[Developmental Stages]]).

---

## Causes and Mechanisms  
### Biological Basis  
- **Evolutionary Perspective**: Evolutionary psychologists propose that stranger anxiety evolved as a protective mechanism to keep infants close to caregivers in potentially dangerous situations (Bowlby, 1969; [[Attachment Theory]]).  
- **Neurological Development**: Linked to maturation of the **amygdala** (fear response) and **prefrontal cortex** (social evaluation) (Tottenham et al., 2010).  

### Psychological Factors  
- **Attachment Security**: Securely attached infants ([[Attachment Theory#Secure Attachment]]) may exhibit milder stranger anxiety compared to insecurely attached peers (Ainsworth, 1978).  
- **Temperament**: Children with "slow-to-warm-up" temperaments often display prolonged stranger anxiety (Kagan, 1984).

> [!TIP]- Practical Implication  
> Parents in communal societies often mitigate stranger anxiety through **shared caregiving** ([[Cultural Childrearing Practices]]).

## Historical Context  
- **Freudian Theory**: Early psychoanalysts attributed stranger anxiety to fear of maternal separation (Freud, 1926).  
- **Modern Shift**: Contemporary research emphasizes **cognitive appraisal** (e.g., Sroufe, 1995) and [[Social Referencing]] as mediators.  

---

## Controversies and Debates  
- **Universality Debate**: Some argue stranger anxiety is less pronounced in cultures with extended family systems ([[Cultural Childrearing Practices]] vs. [[Western Parenting Norms]]).  
- **Measurement Issues**: Reliance on parental reports vs. lab-based observations (e.g., [[Ainsworth’s Strange Situation]]).
