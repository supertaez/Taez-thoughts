# Notes on Matt Haig's Quote: *Don't Absorb Criticism from People You Wouldn't Go to for Advice*

> **"Don’t absorb criticism from people you wouldn’t go to for advice."**  
> — Matt Haig [[1]]

This quote is a powerful reminder of the importance of filtering external opinions and aligning them with your values. It comes from Matt Haig’s widely acclaimed book *The Comfort Book*, which serves as a collection of reflections aimed at helping readers navigate life’s challenges [[3]]. Below, we explore this quote extensively through its meaning, applications, key takeaways, connections to other works, practical activities, and resources.

---

## Summary/Plot/Meaning

Matt Haig emphasizes the need to be selective about whose opinions we internalize. The quote suggests that not all criticism holds weight—only feedback from those who genuinely understand us or have earned our trust should influence how we perceive ourselves or our actions. By rejecting unconstructive criticism, we protect our mental well-being and focus on what truly matters [[4]].

In essence, it encourages mindfulness when evaluating others’ words. If someone isn’t qualified to give you advice (based on their knowledge, experience, or intentions), then their critique likely lacks value.

---

## Key Takeaways and Learnings

### 1. **Filter Feedback Wisely**
   - Not every opinion deserves space in your mind.
   - Assess whether the person offering criticism has insight into your situation or goals.

### 2. **Protect Your Mental Space**
   - Allowing irrelevant criticism can lead to unnecessary stress and self-doubt.
   - Prioritize constructive input over negativity.

### 3. **Build Confidence in Your Judgment**
   - Recognizing the difference between helpful guidance and baseless judgment empowers you to make better decisions.

### 4. **Align with Trusted Advisors**
   - Surround yourself with people whose perspectives align with your values and aspirations.

---

## Practical Applications in Daily Life

### Daily Habits
1. **Morning Reflection:** Start each day by reminding yourself of your trusted circle. Ask yourself, “Who do I respect enough to listen to?”
2. **Criticism Journal:** Whenever you receive feedback, jot it down. Categorize it as either constructive (from trusted sources) or dismissible (from unreliable critics).
3. **Evening Review:** Reflect on interactions where you felt criticized. Evaluate whether the source was credible and actionable.

### Objectives
- Develop emotional resilience against unwarranted criticism.
- Strengthen relationships with mentors and advisors who uplift you.

### Activities
- **Role Play Scenarios:** Practice responding politely but firmly to unsolicited criticism. For example, say, “Thank you for sharing, but I’ll consider advice from those closer to my journey.”
- **Create a ‘Trust Circle’:** Identify 3–5 individuals whose opinions matter most to you. Keep this list visible (e.g., on your phone or desk).

### Assessments
- Weekly Check-In: Track moments when you successfully filtered out irrelevant criticism versus times you absorbed it unnecessarily.
