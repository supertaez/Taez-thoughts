# The Fourth Turning & Strauss-Howe Generational Theory  
![](https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1685651696i/670089.jpg) 

> [!quote]  
> "History is seasonal, and winter is coming."  
> — William Strauss & Neil Howe  

## Summary  
*The Fourth Turning* presents a cyclical theory of history driven by generational archetype rotation. Strauss and Howe argue that modern Anglo-American history follows a consistent 80-100 year cycle (a *saeculum*) divided into four distinct 20-25 year periods called "turnings." Each turning represents a generational shift with profound impacts on society, culture, economics, and politics.

The authors emphasize that societies move through these four distinct seasons—High (spring), Awakening (summer), Unraveling (autumn), and Crisis (winter)—with remarkable consistency across centuries. The most important phase, the Fourth Turning (Crisis), represents a decisive era when institutional life is destroyed and rebuilt in response to a perceived threat to the nation's survival.

According to the authors' timeline, we are currently in a Fourth Turning crisis period that began with the 2008 financial collapse and will likely culminate in a climactic resolution by approximately 2030. The 2023 update *The Fourth Turning Is Here* reinforces their view that recent events (pandemic, political polarization, economic instability) confirm their predictions.

### The Four Turnings Cycle:

> [!info]  
> Each saeculum contains four turnings, each with a distinct societal mood and purpose.

| Turning | Societal Mood | Purpose | Recent Example |
|---------|---------------|---------|---------------|
| **First Turning (High)** | Confident, strong institutions, collective harmony | Building & Prosperity | Post-WWII America (1946-1964) |
| **Second Turning (Awakening)** | Passionate attack on institutions, spiritual searching | Cultural Revolution | Consciousness Revolution (1964-1984) |
| **Third Turning (Unraveling)** | Weakening institutions, individualism, cynicism | Cultural Fragmentation | Culture Wars, Neoliberalism (1984-2008) |
| **Fourth Turning (Crisis)** | Institutional collapse, civic authority, collective sacrifice | Destruction & Renewal | Great Recession → Present (2008-~2030) |

### The Four Generational Archetypes:

> [!info]  
> Each generation embodies one of four archetypes that shape their values and behaviors.

| Archetype | Born During | Key Traits | Current Example |
|-----------|-------------|------------|----------------|
| **Prophet** | After Crisis (High) | Idealistic, moralistic, values-focused | Baby Boomers (b. 1943-1960) |
| **Nomad** | After Awakening | Pragmatic, liberty-seeking, survivalist | Generation X (b. 1961-1981) |
| **Hero** | After Unraveling | Civic-minded, community-focused, collective | Millennials (b. 1982-2004) |
| **Artist** | During Crisis | Adaptive, consensus-building, pluralistic | Generation Z (b. 2005-present) |

## Key Themes & Concepts

### 1. The Saeculum
The saeculum is the ~80-100 year cycle that encompasses one complete rotation of generational archetypes and turnings. This approximates the maximum human lifespan, ensuring that living memory of the previous Crisis fades as the next Crisis approaches—a crucial element that allows history to rhyme rather than repeat exactly.

> [!quote]  
> "A saeculum is the length of a long human life, roughly eighty to one hundred years. It is divided into four 'turnings,' each lasting roughly twenty to twenty-five years."

### 2. Generational Archetypes 
The theory contends that generations develop collective personalities based on when they come of age in relation to the turning cycle:

- **Prophet Generation (Boomers)**: Grow up during a High, come of age during an Awakening. Focused on values, vision, and spiritual renewal. Often become moral crusaders and ideological leaders.

- **Nomad Generation (Gen X)**: Grow up during an Awakening, come of age during an Unraveling. Pragmatic, liberty-focused, and resilient. Often become practical problem-solvers and tough leaders during Crisis periods.

- **Hero Generation (Millennials)**: Grow up during an Unraveling, come of age during a Crisis. Civic-minded, optimistic, and team-oriented. Often become the young troops and builders who implement solutions during and after a Crisis.

- **Artist Generation (Gen Z)**: Born during a Crisis, come of age during a High. Adaptive, consensus-building, and sensitive. Often become the technocrats and administrators who maintain systems established after a Crisis.

### 3. Historical Pattern Recognition
The authors identify consistent patterns across American history:

| Saeculum | Crisis (4th Turning) | High (1st Turning) | Awakening (2nd Turning) | Unraveling (3rd Turning) |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------|--------------------------|-----------------------|
| **Colonial** | Glorious Revolution (1675-1704) | Augustan Age of Empire (1704-1727) | Great Awakening (1727-1746) | French & Indian Wars (1746-1773) |
| **Revolutionary** | American Revolution (1773-1794) | Era of Good Feelings (1794-1822) | Transcendental Awakening (1822-1844) | Mexican War & Sectionalism (1844-1860) |
| **Civil War** | Civil War & Reconstruction (1860-1865) | Reconstruction & Gilded Age (1865-1886) | Third Great Awakening (1886-1908) | World War I & Prohibition (1908-1929) |
| **Millennial** | Great Depression & WWII (1929-1946) | American High (1946-1964) | Consciousness Revolution (1964-1984) | Culture Wars & Neoliberalism (1984-2008) |
| **Digital** | Great Recession, Pandemic, ? (2008-???) | ? | ? | ? |

## Historical Evidence & Examples

### Past Fourth Turnings in American History:
1. **American Revolution (1773-1794)**
   - Crisis catalyst: Boston Tea Party
   - Climax: Revolutionary War
   - Resolution: Constitutional Convention

2. **Civil War Era (1860-1865)**
   - Crisis catalyst: Election of Lincoln/Southern secession
   - Climax: Civil War
   - Resolution: Reconstruction

3. **Great Depression & WWII (1929-1946)**
   - Crisis catalyst: Stock Market Crash
   - Climax: World War II
   - Resolution: Post-war international order

4. **Current Crisis (2008-~2030)**
   - Crisis catalyst: Financial Crisis of 2008
   - Potential climax: Pandemic aftermath, geopolitical conflict, or climate crisis
   - Resolution: Yet to be determined

> [!quote]  
> "The reward at the end of the Fourth Turning is that problems are solved, decisions are made, and the table is cleared for a new saeculum to begin."

### International Historical Parallels:
The authors primarily focus on Anglo-American history but acknowledge similar patterns exist in other societies:

- **Ancient Rome**: The cyclical rise and fall of the Roman Republic followed similar patterns
- **Medieval/Early Modern Europe**: Religious upheaval and state formation followed cyclical patterns
- **East Asia**: Dynastic cycles in China reflect similar generational patterns

## Critical Analysis & Scholarly Reception

### Strengths of the Theory:
1. Provides compelling explanations for historical patterns
2. Successfully predicted many aspects of post-2008 societal developments
3. Offers actionable frameworks for navigating periods of instability
4. Integrates multiple disciplines (history, sociology, economics, psychology)
5. Resonates with observed generational differences

### Criticisms of the Theory:
1. **Determinism**: Critics argue the theory is overly deterministic and doesn't account for human agency
2. **Anglo-centrism**: Primary focus on American/British history limits global applicability
3. **Cherry-picking**: Some argue that historical events are selectively emphasized to fit the pattern
4. **Confirmation bias**: Events can be interpreted to fit the framework retrospectively
5. **Political appropriation**: The theory has been embraced by some political figures for ideological purposes

> [!quote]  
> "I don't think there's any way you can get around the fact that the generational approach is a form of determinism."  
> — Critics of the Strauss-Howe theory

### Academic Reception:
The theory has received mixed reception in academic circles. While some historians find value in the cyclical approach, mainstream academic historians tend to be skeptical of grand cyclical theories that appear deterministic. The theory has gained more traction in business, policy, and futurist circles than in traditional academic settings.

## Quotes & Analysis

> [!quote]  
> "People don't make history; history makes people. Generations are formed by history, by the era in which you were born and came of age."

**Analysis**: This captures the core deterministic element of the theory - that historical context shapes collective generational consciousness more than individual choices.

> [!quote]  
> "The Fourth Turning is a Crisis, a decisive era of secular upheaval, when the values regime propels the replacement of the old civic order with a new one."

**Analysis**: Defines the critical phase where institutional transformation occurs, emphasizing that Fourth Turnings aren't simply difficult times but transformational periods where social contracts are rewritten.

> [!quote]  
> "A Crisis arises in response to sudden threats that previously would have been ignored or deferred, but which are now perceived as dire. Great worldly perils boil off the clutter and complexity of life, leaving behind one simple imperative: The society must prevail."

**Analysis**: Explains how Fourth Turning crises create clarity of purpose and collective action that weren't possible during the individualistic Unraveling phase.

> [!quote]  
> "History is seasonal, and winter is coming."

**Analysis**: The authors' most famous quote emphasizes the inevitability of the cyclical pattern and the importance of preparation for difficult times.

## Conclusion: Navigating the Current Fourth Turning

According to Strauss and Howe's updated work, we are currently in the midst of a Fourth Turning that began with the 2008 financial crisis. The COVID-19 pandemic, increasing political polarization, and institutional failures represent the acceleration of this Crisis period.

The authors predict a climax around 2025-2030, followed by a resolution that will establish new institutions and social contracts. While they emphasize that the specific events cannot be predicted, the pattern of destruction and renewal is predictable.

> [!important]  
> The key to navigating a Fourth Turning is understanding your generational role while preparing for significant institutional transformation.

As with previous Fourth Turnings, the current crisis will likely result in:
1. Significant restructuring of economic systems
2. Changes to the social contract between citizens and government
3. Transformation of international alliances and power structures
4. New civic institutions that reflect post-crisis values
5. A generational realignment of political power

The authors maintain that while Fourth Turnings involve tremendous upheaval and sacrifice, they ultimately serve as necessary renewal mechanisms for society, clearing away dysfunctional institutions and establishing new frameworks better suited to emerging challenges.
