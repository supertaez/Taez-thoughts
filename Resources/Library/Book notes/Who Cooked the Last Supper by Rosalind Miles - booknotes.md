# Who Cooked the Last Supper: The Women's History of the World by Rosalind Miles

!["Who Cooked the Last Supper?" book cover](https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/I/51J8G7XQZ8L._SY445_SX342_.jpg)

---

## Summary/Plot
Rosalind Miles' *Who Cooked the Last Supper?* (1988/2001) challenges traditional male-dominated historical narratives by centering women's contributions to global history. Spanning prehistoric societies to 20th-century feminism, Miles argues that women's roles in survival, culture, and revolution have been systematically erased. 

### Core Structure:
- **Part I: In the Beginning Was Woman** - Prehistoric societies and the rise of matriarchal cultures
- **Part II: The Great Goddess** - Ancient goddess worship and the transition to patriarchal religions
- **Part III: The Rise of the Phallus** - The establishment of male-dominated societies
- **Part IV: Husbands and Wives** - Marriage as an institution of control
- **Part V: The Long March** - Women's struggle for rights and recognition

Key themes include:
- **Prehistoric Matriarchies**: Rejects "Man the Hunter" myth, emphasizing women's role in food gathering, agriculture invention, and community care
- **Religious Patriarchy**: Traces the suppression of goddess worship through monotheistic religions like Christianity, Judaism, and Islam
- **Economic Marginalization**: Examines how industrialization segregated women from public life and devalued domestic labor
- **Modern Feminism**: Critiques 20th-century feminism's lack of intersectionality while celebrating grassroots activism
- **Cultural Revolution**: Documents women's role in art, literature, science, and social movements

---

## Key Takeaways & Learnings
1. **Systemic Erasure**: History is written by men, sidelining women's intellectual, cultural, and labor contributions across all civilizations
2. **Patriarchy's Roots**: Monotheism and colonialism institutionalized gender hierarchies by demonizing femininity and goddess worship
3. **Economic Foundation**: Women's unpaid labor has been the invisible foundation of all economic systems
4. **Intersectional Gaps**: Early feminist movements often excluded women of color, working-class women, and non-Western experiences
5. **Cultural Resistance**: Women's resilience—from ancient priestesses to suffragettes—shaped societal progress despite systematic oppression
6. **Ongoing Struggle**: Gender equality requires dismantling cultural norms, religious dogma, and economic structures, not just legal barriers
7. **Archaeological Evidence**: Emerging evidence supports the existence of egalitarian and matriarchal societies before patriarchal dominance
8. **Language and Power**: The evolution of language reflects the shift from female-centered to male-centered worldviews

## Notable Quotes & Ideas

### On Women's Origins and Evolution
- **"The story of the human race begins with a female."**  
  Miles opens by asserting women's centrality to human evolution, challenging Darwinian male-centric narratives.

- **"Woman was the first inventor, the first farmer, the first doctor, the first diplomat."**  
  Emphasizes women's foundational role in developing civilization's most crucial innovations.

- **"For hundreds of thousands of years, woman was the dominant sex. Men were her subjects, her inferiors, her property."**  
  Challenges the assumption that male dominance is natural or historical.

### On Historical Erasure
- **"Men wrote history; women lived it."**  
  Highlights the disconnect between recorded history and women's lived experiences.

- **"The exclusion of women from history is not accidental but systematic, not occasional but permanent."**  
  Exposes the deliberate nature of women's historical invisibility.

- **"Every woman who has ever lived has been told that her contribution to the human race was inferior to that of men. This is the greatest lie ever told."**

### On Religion and Patriarchy
- **"The God of the Christians, Jews, and Muslims is a male god, and his maleness is not incidental but central to his power."**

- **"Before God the Father, there was God the Mother. The Great Goddess reigned supreme for thousands of years before the coming of the masculine God."**

- **"The demonization of the feminine principle in religion was the single most effective weapon in the war against women."**

### On Marriage and Control
- **"Marriage was not made in heaven, but in the council chambers of men who needed to control women's sexuality and reproduction."**

- **"The wedding ring is the smallest handcuff ever invented."**

- **"Love had nothing to do with marriage until very recently in human history, and even now, love is often the casualty of the institution."**

### On Women's Work and Economics
- **"Women's work has never been valued because it has never been visible to those who make the valuations - men."**

- **"Housework is the only occupation in which a person works 15-16 hours a day for no wages, no holidays, no sick leave, and no recognition."**

- **"The Industrial Revolution was not a revolution for women - it was a counterrevolution that pushed them back into the home."**

### On Modern Feminism
- **"Feminism is not about making women stronger - women are already strong. It's about changing the way the world perceives that strength."**

- **"The personal is political, and the political is personal."**

- **"The kyriarchy hurts everyone."**  
  Critiques systems of oppression that harm both women and men.

## Deep Thoughts & Analysis

### Archaeological and Anthropological Evidence
Miles draws heavily on archaeological findings from sites like Çatalhöyük in Turkey and the work of archaeologist Marija Gimbutas. However, her interpretation faces scholarly debate:

- **Goddess Culture Evidence**: Venus figurines, female-centered art, and burial practices suggest reverence for feminine principles in prehistoric societies
- **Criticism**: Some scholars argue Miles overgeneralizes from limited evidence, creating a "golden age" myth
- **Counter-argument**: Recent discoveries continue to support evidence of egalitarian prehistoric societies

### The Transition to Patriarchy
Miles identifies key factors in the shift from goddess worship to male-dominated societies:

1. **Agricultural Revolution**: The need to control reproduction and inheritance
2. **Warfare**: Military prowess became valued over nurturing abilities
3. **Religious Reform**: Monotheistic religions systematically replaced goddess worship
4. **Property Laws**: Women became property rather than property owners

### Feminist Methodology Critique
- **Myth of Matriarchal Utopia**: While Miles romanticizes prehistoric goddess cultures, scholars like Cynthia Eller (*The Myth of Matriarchal Prehistory*) question the evidence for universal matriarchies
- **Essentialist Tendencies**: Miles sometimes falls into biological essentialism, suggesting inherent differences between men and women
- **Western-Centric Focus**: Despite global examples, the analysis often centers European and North American experiences

### Literary and Rhetorical Analysis
- **Feminist Humor**: Miles uses sarcasm and irony to make heavy topics accessible, a technique mirrored in Caitlin Moran's *How to Be a Woman*
- **Narrative Structure**: The chronological approach mirrors traditional history while subverting its content
- **Provocative Title**: The Last Supper reference immediately challenges Christian male-centered narratives

### Contemporary Relevance
Miles' work anticipated many current feminist discussions:
- **#MeToo Movement**: Echoes her analysis of systematic sexual violence
- **Reproductive Rights**: Her critique of religious control over women's bodies remains relevant
- **Economic Inequality**: The gender pay gap reflects her analysis of devalued women's work
- **Intersectionality**: Though limited in her own analysis, she laid groundwork for more inclusive feminism

### Methodological Innovations
- **Interdisciplinary Approach**: Combines archaeology, anthropology, history, and religious studies
- **Global Perspective**: Draws examples from cultures worldwide, though with limitations
- **Popular Accessibility**: Makes academic feminist theory accessible to general readers

## Criticisms and Scholarly Debates

### Academic Reception
- **Positive**: Praised for accessibility and challenging male-dominated historical narratives
- **Mixed**: Criticized for overgeneralization and lack of rigorous source analysis
- **Negative**: Dismissed by some historians for "feminist wishful thinking"

### Key Criticisms
1. **Methodological Issues**: Cherry-picking evidence to support predetermined conclusions
2. **Essentialist Arguments**: Suggesting inherent gender differences that may reinforce stereotypes
3. **Historical Accuracy**: Some claims about prehistoric societies lack sufficient archaeological support
4. **Cultural Universalism**: Applying Western feminist frameworks to non-Western cultures inappropriately

### Scholarly Responses
- **Gerda Lerner** (*The Creation of Patriarchy*): Offers more rigorous historical methodology
- **Cynthia Eller** (*The Myth of Matriarchal Prehistory*): Directly challenges Miles' prehistoric claims
- **Bell hooks** (*Feminist Theory*): Addresses intersectionality gaps in early feminist works
