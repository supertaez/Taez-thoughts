## Cooperation vs Competition Theory

**Core Concepts**

- *Cooperation* involves positive interdependence: individuals or groups work together toward shared goals, so one person’s success helps others succeed too[1][6][7].
- *Competition* involves negative interdependence: individuals or groups pursue mutually exclusive goals, so one person’s success reduces the chances of others succeeding[1][6][7].

## Key Differences

| Aspect            | Cooperation                                   | Competition                                  |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Goal Structure    | Goals are linked positively; shared success   | Goals are linked negatively; exclusive win   |
| Communication     | Open, effective, and supportive               | Impaired, guarded, and obstructive           |
| Attitudes         | Friendly, helpful, mutual respect             | Suspicious, desire to dominate, less trust   |
| Outcomes          | Enhanced group satisfaction and productivity  | Focus on individual achievement              |
| Conflict Handling | Seeks mutual solutions                        | Seeks to maximize own gain                   |

## When Each Is Effective

- **Cooperation** is more effective in tasks requiring teamwork, communication, and shared resources, often leading to higher satisfaction and better problem-solving[1][4][7].
- **Competition** can drive individual performance, especially in skill-based or zero-sum situations, but may reduce group cohesion and communication[6].

## Theoretical Foundations

- Developed by Morton Deutsch and expanded by Johnson & Johnson, the theory emphasizes that the type of interdependence (positive for cooperation, negative for competition) shapes group dynamics, communication, and outcomes[1][3][6][7].
- Research shows cooperation generally outperforms competition in group problem-solving, though competition may boost individual drive in certain contexts[4][6].

## Summary

Cooperation and competition are distinct social processes with different effects on group dynamics and outcomes. Cooperation fosters mutual support and shared success, while competition focuses on individual achievement at the potential expense of group cohesion[1][6][7].
