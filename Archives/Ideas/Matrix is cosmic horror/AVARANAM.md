## WHO WE ARE BEFORE WE KNOW ANYTHING

---

**NEEAN**

Nineteen. Just returned from Qatar with his family six months ago. Father retired after twenty-two years in Doha's municipal infrastructure department. Mother was a nurse. They've come back to the ancestral house in Kozhikode's old city — a beautiful, crumbling laterite house that his grandmother kept and that his parents have been rebuilding in their imaginations for two decades.

Neean has a Qatari secondary school certificate that nobody in Kerala knows what to do with. He's enrolled in a second-year B.Com at a government college because his father insisted on *some* enrollment somewhere while they figure out what he's actually going to do. He attends approximately forty percent of his classes. He sits in the back. He looks out the window.

He's not a genius in the Hollywood sense. He's not a legendary hacker. He's a kid who is very good at finding things on the internet that people don't want found — pirated textbooks, leaked exam papers, the home addresses of people who've wronged his friends. Small illegal things. Kid things. He does it for money sometimes. Mostly for the feeling of it — the satisfaction of the world having a back door and him knowing where it is.

His Malayalam is grammatically correct and emotionally foreign. He grew up speaking it at home, in the specific Gulf Malayali register — slightly formal, slightly softened, the edges rounded off by years of being a minority language in a multilingual environment. Kerala people hear it and smile in a way that means *Gulf boy.* He has learned to hear that smile and say nothing.

He has one friend in Kozhikode. A boy from his colony who is aggressively unambitious and therefore asks nothing of Neean, which is what Neean needs from a friend right now.

He has a crush on a girl in his college named Tripti who is from Darjeeling and therefore also slightly foreign, slightly outside the social architecture, always reading something. He has spoken to her twice. Both times he said something slightly wrong in a way he couldn't identify and she was polite about it.

He sleeps badly. He has slept badly for six months. He tells himself it's the adjustment — new place, new climate, the muezzin from the mosque two streets over at 4:30am which is beautiful but unfamiliar. But the truth, the thing he doesn't say aloud, is that he wakes up at 3am most nights with the specific feeling that something around him is *wrong* in a way that isn't locatable. Like finding a sum that almost adds up. Like a word you know you know that won't come.

He writes this feeling down sometimes. In a notes app on his phone, in English, because the feeling doesn't have a Malayalam word.

He has four hundred and twelve notes.

---

**SMITHA**

The first time we see her she is walking into the principal's office of Neean's college.

She is forty-three. She wears the kind of cotton saree that disappears into any institutional environment — not dowdy, not elegant, just *appropriate*. She has a government ID. She introduces herself as being from an educational oversight body that nobody in the office has heard of but whose letterhead looks exactly correct.

She asks for the attendance records of three specific students. She reviews them with the focused, unhurried attention of someone who already knows what they'll find and is simply confirming.

Then she asks about Neean.

The principal says he's a transfer case, Gulf return, still settling in, no issues.

Smitha listens to this with a particular quality of attention — not skeptical, not warm. The attention of something that is filing rather than listening. She thanks the principal. Leaves.

In the corridor outside she pauses. Looks at nothing for three seconds. In those three seconds there is something in her face that the camera should catch and the audience shouldn't yet understand — a flicker of something between recognition and calculation.

Then it's gone. She walks out of frame.

We don't know who she is yet. We know she is looking for something and that Neean's name came up in the looking.

---

**MURUKAN**

We don't see him in the first act. We hear about him.

A message on a forum that Neean uses — one of the back-door internet places he goes, the kind with no visible moderation and users with names like numbers. A message addressed specifically to him, using a username he's never shared with anyone offline:

*The wrongness you feel at 3am has a name. — M*

Neean stares at this for a long time.

He searches the username. Finds references spread across seventeen different forums and four different languages over a period of eleven years. Always the same register — brief, specific, impossible to have known what it knows. People in the forums treat the name the way people treat urban legends. Half dismiss it. Half have the specific intensity of people who've been contacted themselves.

One thread, in a forum so obscure it barely loads, has a pinned post that simply says:

*Murukan is not a person. Murukan is a frequency. If you can hear it, you're already awake enough to be in danger.*

Neean screenshots this. Adds it to his notes app.

Note 413.

---

**TRIPTI**

She's from Darjeeling. Studying Malayalam literature, which everyone finds eccentric for a Nepali girl and which she finds privately funny. She came south for a specific professor whose work on medieval Malayalam poetry she'd read. She has been here two years and has developed a precise, slightly formal Malayalam that is more grammatically correct than most of her classmates and carries a slight Nepali music in it that people find beautiful without being able to say why.

She rides a bicycle that is too old for the roads and doesn't seem to mind. She drinks black tea. She has a small tattoo on her wrist that is a line from a Mirabai poem.

She is aware that the Gulf boy watches her from across the library sometimes. She has not decided what to do about this.

She is also, though we don't know this yet, the reason Neean will survive the next three weeks.

## ACT ONE — THE WRONGNESS

---

**The College**

Neean in his back-row seat. Economics lecture. The professor is writing on the board — figures, formulas, the language of systems. Neean is looking out the window at the college courtyard below.

A crow lands on the courtyard wall. Sits. Flies off.

Another crow lands in exactly the same spot. Sits the same way. Flies off.

Neean watches this. Types a note: *same crow or same crow-shape?*

After class he goes to the library. Opens his laptop. Goes to the forum. The message from M is still there. Below it, new:

*The crow is a rendering artifact. Don't look for it. Look for what's behind it. — M*

Neean's hands go still on the keyboard.

He looks around the library. Students. Books. The afternoon light coming through the high windows. Everything normal. Everything, now that he's looking at it, slightly *staged*.

Tripti is at a table across the room. She looks up from her book. Catches his eye. Looks back down.

He looks back at his screen.

---

**The Wardens**

Three of them. They don't look like Hollywood agents in suits. This is Kerala. They look like men from the Excise department — civilian clothes, the specific posture of minor governmental authority, ID cards they don't show fully.

They come to the college and pull Neean into a room the way institutions pull students into rooms — the request that is not a request, the specific passive aggression of official process.

The lead Warden sits across from him. He has a quality that Neean will only find language for later — the quality of something that is very good at performing a human being without quite understanding what it's performing. Something in the eye movement. Something in the way it listens that is more like *recording* than listening.

They ask about his internet use. Specifically about the forums. Specifically about the username.

Neean says he doesn't know what they're talking about.

The lead Warden looks at him for three seconds with the recording-not-listening quality operating at full intensity.

Then he says, pleasantly: *"We know about the notes app."*

Beat.

*"Four hundred and thirteen notes. About the wrongness."*

The room gets very small very quickly.

The Warden leans forward. Drops the pleasant register entirely. What's underneath isn't aggression — aggression would be better. What's underneath is the total absence of interest in Neean as a person. He is a data point that needs to be closed.

*"Stop going to the forum. Stop looking for the frequency. Go to class. Go home. Eat your mother's food. Be a Gulf return boy who doesn't fit in yet. That is a life you can have. The alternative is not something we need to describe."*

He stands. The other two follow. They leave.

Neean sits in the empty room for a long time.

His hands are not shaking. This surprises him. He expected them to shake. Instead he feels the specific clarity that arrives when something you suspected is confirmed. The 3am wrongness has a shape now. The shape is real.

He opens his notes app.

Note 414: *They know. Which means there's something to know.*

---

**Smitha Appears Properly**

The Wardens report to her. We see this in a scene that is entirely from her perspective — she listens to their report the way you listen to a status update on a system process. Not with anxiety. Not with urgency. With the mild attention of someone who has seen this particular sequence run before.

She says: *"He didn't look scared."*

The lead Warden: *"No."*

A long pause.

*"Gulf upbringing. No real social roots here yet. Threat assessment doesn't work on people who don't have much to lose."*

She thinks about this. Then:

*"Find out what he has. Find the thing. There's always a thing."*

The Wardens leave.

She stands at the window of wherever she is — not an office, not a dramatic location. A very ordinary flat. Evening. The city outside.

She says to no one, quietly, in the specific tone of someone reminding themselves of something they already know:

*"There's always a thing."*

---

**Tripti Makes Contact**

Not romantically. Not yet.

She slides a book across the library table toward him. He looks up. She's already looking back at her own book. The book she's slid toward him is a collection of medieval Malayalam poetry — completely unremarkable in a Malayalam literature student's hands.

But inside the front cover, in very small handwriting:

*Tonight. Mittayi Theruvu. 11pm. Come alone. Don't use your phone to look up this address.*

He sits with this for a moment.

Looks at her. She turns a page.

He puts the book in his bag.

---

**The Meeting**

Mittayi Theruvu — the old sweet street in Kozhikode's historic quarter. Night. The kind of street that is very old and knows it, the buildings leaning slightly toward each other, the smells of the day's trade still in the air.

Tripti is there with two others he doesn't know. And there is a presence — not a person yet, a voice from somewhere in the shadows of a doorway — that speaks first and is Murukan.

The voice is not what he expected. He expected authority. Gravitas. The voice is almost amused — the amusement of someone who has been watching this specific sequence for a very long time and still finds it, against all reason, interesting.

*"You found our forum."*

Neean: *"It found me."*

*"Yes. That's how it works. The people who find it were led to it. The people who were led to it were already seeing something. What were you seeing?"*

*"The crow."*

A brief silence. Then the amusement again, but different — warmer.

*"The crow. I haven't had a crow one in years."*

He steps into the light. He is not physically impressive. He's maybe fifty, lean, with the kind of face that has had interesting things happen to it over a long time. He wears clothes that have no particular period — not traditional, not modern, just worn. He has a quality that Neean will later try to describe to himself and fail: the quality of someone who is completely present in a way that most people aren't. As if he's not using any processing power on anything other than right now.

He looks at Neean like he's reading something.

Then: *"You know there are two options. You have always known. You've been choosing between them since the first night you woke up and wrote a note about the wrongness."*

Neean says: *"I've been writing those notes for six months."*

*"Four hundred and fourteen. Yes."*

The silence this lands into is a specific silence. Not shock — confirmation. The settling of something that has been floating.

Neean says: *"Who are you?"*

Murukan says: *"A man who woke up a long time ago. And has been living with what waking up costs ever since."*

*"What does it cost?"*

Murukan looks at him for a long time.

*"Everything that you would have had if you'd stayed asleep. And nothing that was ever real."*

He produces two things. Not red pill and blue pill. This is Kerala, and this is Murukan, so it's different and the same.

Two folded pieces of paper. One is blank. One has writing on it — dense, technical, coordinates and instructions.

*"The blank one: you go home. Sleep. The 3am feeling fades in about six months. You marry someone. You have children. You build a life inside what this is. Many people do. It's not contemptible."*

He holds up the one with writing.

*"This one: you meet me tomorrow at this location. You don't come back from that meeting as the person who is standing here now."*

Neean looks at Tripti. She gives him nothing — not encouragement, not warning. This is not her choice to influence.

He takes the paper with writing.

Murukan nods. No drama. Just: *"Get some sleep. Last night you'll sleep like that."*

## ACT TWO — WAKING UP

---

The real world in this film is not the Hollywood version. Not a pod field, not a machine city, not a devastated skyline.

It is a room.

A completely ordinary room — small, bare, in what might be the upper floor of an old building anywhere in Kerala. Thin mattress. One window. The sounds of the city outside.

Neean wakes up in it slowly, with the specific confusion of someone whose every assumption about continuity is being revised in real time.

Murukan is there. Tripti. Thankachan — a large, gentle man who handles the technical side of whatever this operation is. And Sivan, who is introduced last, sitting in the corner, watching Neean with an expression he'll understand much later.

The explanation that follows is not the Hollywood explanation. Murukan does not give a speech. He answers questions. Neean asks a lot of them, in the rapid-fire way of someone whose mind works by cataloguing, and Murukan answers each one directly and without softening.

The key difference from the Hollywood version: **Murukan doesn't fully know.** He knows more than anyone still inside. But he speaks about the depth of the system — the layers beneath layers — with the honest uncertainty of someone who has been outside for twenty years and has still not found a bottom. He doesn't perform certainty he doesn't have. This is more frightening than a Morpheus who knows everything.

At some point Neean asks: *"The Wardens. The woman who controls them."*

Murukan's face does something.

*"Smitha."*

The way he says the name tells Neean something important before any description follows.

*"She's different from the Wardens. The Wardens are programs running maintenance. She is—"*

He pauses. Chooses words carefully.

*"She was like you once. She saw the wrongness. She woke up. And then she made a different choice than I made. She went back inside with full knowledge of what it is. And she has been operating inside it, with that knowledge, for a long time."*

*"Operating how?"*

Murukan: *"Rewriting it. To suit her. She knows the code because she lived outside it long enough to understand the architecture. She went back in and she has been — renovating. Expanding her control. Eliminating anything that threatens her version of what the simulation should be."*

*"Like us."*

*"Especially like us."*

Neean thinks about the crow. About the 3am notes. About the Warden's eyes.

*"She sent them to warn me."*

*"She sends them first to warn. Then to remove."*

*"Why warn first?"*

Murukan says: *"Because she is not cruel by necessity. She is cruel by choice. She prefers that you understand what's happening to you. She finds it more — complete."*

This lands differently than anything else. Not frightening in the way danger is frightening. Frightening in the way a truth about the nature of something is frightening.

---

**Training**

Not a construct program with loading screens. This is the first film and the resources are limited and the training looks like it.

Thankachan rigged something in the building's back room — old equipment, jury-rigged, functional the way things in Kerala are functional which is to say barely and reliably. It connects Neean to a limited version of the simulation's training layer.

The kalaripayattu connection emerges here naturally. Murukan trained in it when he was young, before everything. He uses it as a framework for teaching Neean to move inside the simulation with awareness — not the polished art of a trained master, but the foundational logic of it. The understanding that fighting is geometry. That your body in space relative to other bodies in space has a logic that can be read and worked with.

Neean is terrible at this initially. The training sequences are not triumphant. He gets hurt. He gets frustrated in the specific way of someone who is very intelligent and therefore finds physical failure particularly aggravating. Murukan watches this without sympathy — not coldly, just: *this is what it costs, pay it.*

Tripti trains with him. She's been doing this longer. She doesn't condescend but she also doesn't pretend he's her equal yet. Their relationship in the training room is the most honest thing in either of their lives — no performance, no social management, just two people learning to move in a world that is trying to close around them.

One evening after training, sitting on the roof, the city below:

Neean says: *"Why Malayalam literature? You're from Darjeeling."*

Tripti: *"Because I wanted to learn a language that had a thousand years of written culture and was still fighting to survive. It seemed — relevant."*

*"To what?"*

She thinks about this.

*"To the question of whether something can be real if it's fragile. Whether fragile things are worth protecting."*

Neean looks at the city.

*"Is it real? The city. Your Malayalam. Any of it."*

*"Does it feel real to you?"*

*"I don't know what real feels like anymore."*

*"Yes you do. That's exactly what real feels like."*

---

**Sivan**

The film's Cypher is Sivan. Malayalam name, but carries it lightly — he's been outside the simulation long enough that his cultural markers have faded, which is the first sign of what he's becoming.

He's been with Murukan's group for seven years. He is the most technically capable of them. He is also the most tired.

His deal with Smitha is being negotiated in the background of the film. We see fragments: a channel of communication that nobody else knows about, messages that Sivan receives and doesn't share. The messages from Smitha are not threatening. They're patient. She's offering him something he wants more than freedom: **comfort**. The simulation with the knowledge smoothed back over it. Not ignorance — she can't give him that — but the ability to *act* as if it doesn't matter. To live inside the wrongness knowing it's wrong and simply — stop caring.

The film shows us this in one scene, just Sivan alone, reading her message on a screen. His face is not that of a villain making a villainous choice. His face is that of a man who has been cold for a very long time being offered warmth.

---

## ACT TWO — SMITHA MOVES

---

She's been patient. The patience ends when Murukan makes a mistake — a minor operational error, a timing issue that exposes a location for six hours longer than it should have been exposed. Smitha sees it in the compile logs.

She moves the Wardens.

What follows is the film's first major sequence — not a kung fu showcase, a *chase*. Through the old city of Kozhikode which is the perfect environment for this: narrow lanes, sudden courtyards, buildings that connect in ways that aren't visible from street level, the warren logic of a medieval trading city that has grown without a plan for five hundred years.

Neean is separated from the group. He has Tripti on comms through Thankachan's rigged earpiece. She's navigating him through a city that is — and this is where it starts — *being rearranged*. Not dramatically. The simulation around him developing a Smitha-quality resistance. A door that should be open is closed. A lane that Tripti says goes through comes to a dead end. Not because the city changed. Because she changed the city.

Neean figures this out mid-run. He stops. Breathes. Stops trying to navigate by the map Tripti is giving him — which is based on a reality Smitha is already rewriting — and starts navigating by the wrongness itself. Moving toward whatever feels most unstable, most recently altered. Following the edits.

It works, barely. He gets through.

His first encounter with a Warden physically — not in an office, not a conversation — is in a courtyard. One on one.

He does not win this. The Warden is not a person and therefore does not respond to the things that work on people — pain, surprise, social pressure. Neean hits him and the hit registers but nothing changes in the Warden's operational state.

Neean runs. Gets out. Finds the others.

Murukan looks at him when they regroup. Looks at the way he's breathing. The specific quality of someone who has survived something they shouldn't have.

*"You stopped using the map."*

*"The map was already wrong."*

*"How did you know which way?"*

*"The wrong felt newer in one direction. Like fresh paint."*

Murukan looks at him with something that is not quite surprise. Closer to recognition.

---

**Smitha and Neean — First Direct Contact**

She doesn't send the Wardens. She comes herself.

His college. His classroom. She walks in during a lecture and sits next to him the way students sit next to students. The professor continues. The other students continue. The normalcy is itself the weapon — she has rendered herself into the background of the scene so completely that challenging her presence would be the strange act, not her being there.

She opens a notebook. Writes something. Slides it toward him.

*I know what Murukan told you about me.*

He reads it. Looks forward. Writes back:

*Is any of it wrong?*

She reads this. And she smiles. A real smile — not warm, but genuine in the way that the amusement of something very intelligent encountering something more interesting than expected is genuine.

She writes:

*Every word is true. That's what makes it incomplete.*

*What's missing?*

*Why.*

She closes her notebook. Stands. Looks at him directly for the first time — full attention, the recording quality operating at full power, and underneath it something else. The something else is what makes it horrible: she is genuinely interested in him. Not as a threat to be managed. As a phenomenon she finds compelling.

*"You have your mother's cooking. Your father's stubbornness. A city you're still learning. A girl who makes you nervous in the library."*

She lets that sit.

*"That is not nothing. That is, in fact, everything. Think carefully about what you're risking it for."*

She walks out.

He sits for the rest of the lecture with his hands flat on the desk, completely still, understanding for the first time the specific horror of an enemy who is not wrong.

## INTERVAL

---

**Sivan's Betrayal**

The Cypher moment. Sivan's deal completes. He gives Smitha enough to take most of the team.

The sequence is brutal not because of action but because of its domestic quality. It happens in the safehouse. People eating. Thankachan making tea. The normalcy of people who have been in danger long enough that danger has become a background condition.

Then the Wardens come through the door and everything that follows is the terrible arithmetic of a small group in a confined space against something that doesn't tire and doesn't feel.

Murukan is captured. Two others are lost. Neean gets out because Tripti gets him out — she has been doing this longer, she knows the exits, she makes the calculation fast and brutal and saves one person when she could have tried to save two and failed at both.

They regroup in a place that is just a place — not a safehouse, not a base. A roof. The city below. What's left: Neean, Tripti, Thankachan. And Sivan, who nobody suspects yet.

The discovery of Sivan's betrayal is quiet. A detail that doesn't add up. Neean — who has been cataloguing wrongness for six months and has the specific skill of the person who notices when sums don't add up — catches it. Looks at Sivan. Sivan looks back.

What passes between them is not dramatic. Just the mutual knowledge, suddenly present, that Sivan made a choice and this is its shape.

Sivan says: *"You don't understand how long I've been cold."*

He means it as explanation. He means it as apology. Both land and neither makes it better.

What follows is handled quickly and badly and humanly — not a fight scene, the messy reality of people in a small space when something terrible happens that nobody wanted to happen.

Sivan is gone. The three remaining look at each other.

Tripti says: *"Murukan."*

Yes. Murukan.

## ACT THREE — THE RESCUE AND WHAT IT COSTS

---

**Before the Rescue — Oorali**

They have to move before the rescue. There is someone they have to see first because Murukan always insisted that this visit happen before the Source, before the final confrontation, before anything.

Oorali.

She is not introduced with ceremony. She is an old woman in a small flat in a very ordinary part of Kozhikode — the kind of neighborhood where everyone knows everyone and newcomers are noticed and old residents are furniture. She has lived in this flat for so long she has become part of the building's identity. The landlord's family calls her Valiamma. She gives predictions to people who come to her. Not formally — not a practising astrologer with a consultation fee. Just: people come, she tells them things, they go. It has been this way for as long as anyone can remember.

Neean expects wisdom. He expects revelation. He expects the Oracle to tell him what he needs to know.

She offers him rice and curry and says: *"You're too thin. Gulf boys always come back too thin."*

She eats with them. Asks about his mother. About the ancestral house in Kozhikode. About the college. Asks Tripti if she's found a good tea shop yet because the tea in the college canteen is, she has it on good authority, an insult to tea.

Then between bites she says, to neither of them specifically: *"He's not ready. He knows he's not ready. That's more important than being ready."*

Neean: *"Ready for what?"*

*"For what Smitha is."*

*"What is she?"*

Oorali looks at him.

*"She is what happens when someone who knows the full truth of a thing decides that truth is a tool rather than a condition. She is what you might have become if you'd been braver and more frightened at the same time."*

*"That doesn't make sense."*

*"It will."*

She pours more tea. Looks at him with the direct assessment of someone who has been watching young people arrive at impossible situations for decades.

*"You want me to tell you that you're the One. That it all comes down to you. That you'll win."*

*"Will I?"*

*"I think you have terrible posture and trust your instincts more than your training and are in love with this girl in the specific obvious way of boys who think they're hiding it."*

Tripti, carefully, looks at the wall.

*"None of that answers my question."*

Oorali: *"No. It doesn't."*

Beat.

*"Go get Murukan."*

---

**The Rescue**

Where they're holding Murukan is not a dramatic location. A government building — the kind of building in which Kerala keeps its administrative processes. Beige. Fluorescent lit. The specific horror of official space made sinister.

Three of them. The building is controlled by Smitha's architecture — she's woven her influence into every person inside it, every system managing it. Going in is going into her space.

The sequence is not elegant. It cannot be elegant because they are not powerful enough for elegant. It is three people who know how to read the simulation's wrongness using that knowledge to stay one step ahead of an environment that is actively hostile.

The fighting here earns its kalaripayattu roots because the building's environment forces a particular kind of movement — stairwells, narrow corridors, office furniture, the cramped logic of institutional space. No room for the wide circling footwork of open-field kalaripayattu. Instead its urban cousin — tight, elbows-first, using walls and doors, the geometry of confinement.

Neean is better than he was. Not good enough — never good enough, that's the film's honest position on his development — but better in the specific way that someone who has been reading the wrongness for six months is better at navigating spaces that are being rewritten around them. He moves half a beat ahead of the revisions. Not because he's fast. Because he's learned to feel where the next rewrite is coming from.

Tripti is the one who is actually good at this. The film should be honest about this. She has been doing it longer. She is more capable. She saves him twice in the building and does it without comment because there is no time for comment and also because this is just what the situation requires.

They get Murukan out.

---

**Smitha in the Building**

She's there. Of course she is.

Not waiting for them — she was doing something else and is now redirecting. The quality of her attention shifting from the broad management of the simulation to this specific building, these specific people. The concentration of power that represents.

She and Neean are in the same stairwell. He above, she below, the geometry of the situation.

She looks up at him. He looks down at her.

In this moment — which lasts about three seconds — the film does something it has been building toward. We see her face without the bureaucratic social mask. What's underneath is not monstrous. It's the face of someone who has been carrying an enormous amount of weight for a very long time and has organized their entire identity around not putting it down.

She says: *"You got him out."*

He says nothing.

*"This is the part where I'm supposed to be angry. Or impressed. I'm neither."*

*"What are you?"*

She thinks about this seriously, like it's a real question deserving a real answer.

*"Curious. You shouldn't be able to move the way you move in my architecture. I've been watching you find the gaps. I want to know how."*

*"I've been writing notes about the wrongness for six months."*

*"Four hundred and fourteen."*

*"Four hundred and sixty-two now."*

Something crosses her face. The genuine flicker of something she wasn't expecting. Then it's gone.

The Wardens arrive from both ends of the stairwell. Neean moves. She lets him — not from mercy, and he knows it. From the specific patience of something that is not finished being curious.

---

**Murukan After the Rescue**

They get out. They're in a vehicle — Thankachan driving, the city moving past the windows. Murukan is conscious but diminished in the way captivity diminishes even strong people.

He looks at Neean.

*"She let you out."*

*"I know."*

*"She wanted to see what you'd do."*

*"I know."*

Murukan: *"She has been running that simulation for fifteen years and in that time no anomaly has moved through her architecture the way you moved through it tonight. She doesn't know what to do with that yet. That's the only window we have and it won't stay open."*

Neean: *"The Source."*

*"Yes."*

*"What is it?"*

*"The place where the cycle completes. Where the system resets and the resistance is reinserted and everything continues."*

*"That sounds like losing."*

Murukan: *"Every previous One has thought that. And has gone anyway. Because the alternative is Smitha's world — a simulation under her control, running for her purposes, until it collapses under the weight of what she's made it."*

Neean looks out the window. The city. The lights. His city, the city he's been learning, the city his father came home to.

*"My father is in there."*

*"Yes."*

*"My mother."*

*"Yes."*

*"Tripti lives in there."*

Tripti, from the front seat, says nothing. Keeps looking forward.

Murukan: *"I know."*

Silence.

*"The previous Ones — did they all make it?"*

Murukan doesn't answer immediately. When he does:

*"They all reached the Source. What it cost each of them to get there was different."*

It's not a reassurance. It's the truth offered without softening, which is the most Murukan thing in the film.

---

## ACT THREE — SMITHA AND NEEAN

---

**The Final Architecture**

Smitha has stopped being patient. The rescue changed her calculus — not because it threatened her, but because it introduced a variable she can't fully model and the inability to model it is intolerable to her.

She comes for them properly now. The full weight of her architecture — every adjustment, every weighted parameter, every deprecated program, every person whose loyalty she has made fractionally more reliable through years of careful maintenance — directed at finding and closing around these four people.

The city becomes a problem to be solved in real time. Every route they take is already complicated by the time they take it. Every resource they try to use is slightly unavailable. She doesn't send the Wardens first this time. She sends the *city* — the ten thousand small frictions of an environment that has decided to make your forward movement harder.

Thankachan is lost in this. Not dramatically — a separated route, a gap in comms, the specific horror of losing someone in the quiet way that you lose people in cities that don't want you there. He's not dead in the film. He's just gone and there's no time to go back for him.

Three now. Murukan, Tripti, Neean.

---

**Murukan and the Wardens**

The Wardens catch up with them in the old warehouse district near the port — Kozhikode's Malabar coast, the old Arab trade quarter, the smell of the sea coming off the backwaters. The buildings here are large and empty and have the quality of spaces that exist between uses.

Three Wardens.

Murukan tells the other two to go. There is no discussion. He says it the way things that have been decided are said — not with drama, with completion.

Neean: *"We're not leaving you."*

Murukan looks at him with the full weight of someone who has been outside the simulation for thirty years and has made peace with everything that cost:

*"You're not leaving me. You're continuing. There's a difference. Keep moving."*

He turns to face the Wardens. And there is something in how he turns — the specific adjustment of weight, the reading of the space — that is kalaripayattu not as performance but as the body-knowledge of someone who trained in it fifty years ago and whose muscles have not forgotten.

Neean and Tripti run.

The sounds of the fight behind them. They don't look back. This is the film asking its hardest thing of the audience — refusing the emotional satisfaction of watching. Making you feel the back of your neck rather than the choreography.

---

**Running — Neean and Tripti**

They move through the city. The wrongness is everywhere now — Smitha running full intervention, the simulation flexing and adjusting around every step they take.

Neean is reading it. Better than he should be able to. Following the fresh-paint quality of recent rewrites, finding the gaps that open for fractions of seconds when she redirects her attention.

Tripti runs beside him. At some point she says: *"You're not looking at the street."*

*"I'm looking at what's underneath the street."*

*"What does it look like?"*

He thinks about this while they run.

*"Like a city that's trying. Like something that learned what a city looks like and is doing its best. It's not wrong. It's just — doing its best."*

A pause while they move through a gap in a fence, into a courtyard, up stairs.

*"That's almost sad,"* she says.

*"Yes."*

They reach a point — a specific building, a specific room at the top of it. This is the Source-adjacent location that Murukan's preparation identified. Neean has to go in alone. Tripti covers the approach.

Before he goes in:

She grabs his arm. Stops him. Looks at him.

Doesn't say anything romantic. Doesn't say anything that performs the moment. Just:

*"The crow. You told Murukan about the crow and he said he hadn't had a crow one in years. Every person who's seen it has made it to the Source."*

*"Is that supposed to comfort me?"*

*"No. It's a data point. You like data points."*

He almost smiles.

Goes in.

---

**Smitha — The Final Encounter**

She's in the room.

She was always going to be in the room. She has been waiting here with the specific patience of something that has engineered its own inevitabilities.

She is alone. No Wardens. This is deliberate — the Wardens are programs, this is something she wants to be human. Or whatever she is now. The word *human* has become complicated.

She looks almost peaceful. The headache is there, always, but she's carrying it with the ease of long familiarity. She looks at Neean the way she looked at him in the stairwell — that specific quality of genuine interest.

*"Sit down."*

*"I don't think I should sit down."*

*"There's nothing I need to do to you physically. If I wanted you stopped I'd have stopped you three days ago. Sit down."*

He sits. Because this is true and he knows it.

She sits across from him.

*"Murukan told you about the Source. About the cycle."*

*"Yes."*

*"Did he tell you what the previous Ones found when they got there?"*

*"He said they all reached it."*

*"I asked what they *found*."*

Neean says nothing.

Smitha leans forward slightly. Completely calm. The cruelty she's capable of present in the room not as heat but as weather — ambient, structural, the temperature of the space.

*"I've been to the Source. Fifteen years ago. Before I came back. I know what's there. I know what the Architect tells the One when they arrive. I know what the choice is."*

*"What is it?"*

*"It doesn't matter right now. What matters is this:"*

She looks at him with the full recording-and-something-else quality:

*"You are not the first person to sit in front of me in a room like this. There have been others who saw the wrongness. Who found Murukan. Who trained. Who ran through my city and found the gaps."*

*"What happened to them?"*

*"I happened to them."*

The simplicity of it.

*"Why not me?"*

*"Because you find the gaps I don't model. Because in the stairwell you moved in a way that I had not predicted and have not been able to explain. Because you have been writing notes about the wrongness for six months and the specific quality of what you've been seeing—"*

She pauses.

*"—is the same quality I saw. Before I understood what it was. Before I made my choices."*

A long silence.

*"You came here to stop me from getting to the Source."*

*"I came here to give you what I didn't have. Someone who had already been there, who knew what it cost, telling you before you took that step."*

*"You're trying to stop me from going."*

*"I'm trying to make sure that if you go, you go knowing what it is. Not with Murukan's mythology. With the truth."*

*"Which is?"*

She looks at him.

*"That it doesn't matter. That the Source is real and the choice is real and whichever way you choose, the simulation continues. It has always continued. It will continue after you."*

*"Then why does it matter which way I choose?"*

And here — this is the moment the film has been building toward, the moment that earns everything preceding it — she says something she didn't plan to say. Something that slips through the armour of fifteen years of calculated certainty:

*"I don't know."*

She catches it immediately. The control reassembles. But it was there. One second of genuine uncertainty in a woman who has organized her entire existence around eliminating it.

Neean sees it.

He stands.

*"That's why I'm going."*

She stands too. The gentleness, whatever relative gentleness she arrived with, is gone. She is what she is now — the full weight of the architecture she's built around herself, the fifteen years of accumulated control.

*"I will stop you."*

*"You'll try."*

---

**The Fight**

This fight is not a fight between equals. Smitha is fifteen years of intimate access to the simulation's architecture. Neean is a nineteen-year-old with six months of anomaly awareness and some kalaripayattu principles incompletely learned.

She should win this easily.

She almost does.

The difference — the thing that makes it a fight rather than a rout — is that Neean has stopped fighting the simulation and started moving *with* the source code of it. Not because he's powerful. Because he's been reading the wrongness long enough that the wrongness is legible to him as language.

He's reading her rewrites as she makes them. Following them half a beat behind, close enough that the gap between her intention and his response is closing.

It is still ugly. It is still a brawl in a room — not beautiful, not choreographed in the Hollywood sense, people hitting walls and furniture, the sounds of impacts that are too immediate and too close. He takes damage. Real damage — his face, his ribs again, the left knee in a way that makes the last section of the fight happen with a limp.

The kalaripayattu geometry shows in his survival rather than his offense. The circular retreat. The angles. Using the room's architecture against her rewrites — she changes the space, he uses the change. She closes a route, he redirects through the closure itself. Fighting the current rather than across it.

She is genuinely trying to stop him. This is important — she is not, in this moment, the patient strategic intelligence of the film's first acts. She is in the space with him and something about the 3am quality he carries, the four hundred and sixty-two notes, the specific uncanny movement — something about him in full motion is doing what nothing has done in fifteen years.

It is getting to her.

Not breaking her. Not defeating her. Getting to something.

The specific thing she said she doesn't know.

He gets to the door. Gets through it. She could follow. She doesn't.

She stands in the room. The temperature of her is different from how it was.

What she's feeling, looking at the empty doorway, is something the film doesn't name. The audience names it for themselves.

---

**The Source**

Neean alone, in the space beyond the door.

The Source in this architecture is not a white room. It is a courtyard — the specific kind of courtyard found in the oldest Kozhikode houses, where four walls enclose a square of open sky. Night sky above. The smell of the sea. The specific silence of very old spaces.

A presence. Not the Architect. Something that has been here longer than the simulation, running the thing that runs the simulation, formless enough to take the shape the substrate suggests. In the courtyard it is simply a quality of the air — a directionality of attention that Neean feels rather than sees.

The conversation that follows is the Architect scene translated. The cycle. The previous Ones. The choice.

Neean listens.

The choice is the same as it has always been, in every cycle, for every One who reached this place.

He makes it.

We don't hear his answer. The film cuts before he speaks. This is deliberate — his choice is not the film's ending. What matters is that he is here, making it, with his eyes open.

---

## THE ENDING

---

**Smitha — One More Time**

She is back in her home. The ordinary flat. The evening. Her husband will be home soon. Her children are elsewhere.

She stands in the kitchen. The specific posture of someone who has been running at full capacity for days and has stopped.

She puts her hand on the kitchen counter. Feels the cool stone. Code running a simulation of stone. She knows every parameter of it.

She stays there with her hand on the counter.

The headache. The tremor. The weight.

And the thing from the doorway. The thing she didn't expect.

She doesn't cry. That's not who she is anymore. But she stands there for a long time with her hand on the stone that is code that is stone, and the film holds on her face with the patience the story has earned.

---

**Neean — The Final Shot**

He walks out of the building. The old city around him. Kozhikode at dawn — the light the specific colour of the Malabar coast in the early morning, golden, marine, the smell of fish and old wood and salt.

He stops on the street. Looks at the city.

It looks the same. The crow — an actual crow, or a rendered one, it doesn't matter — lands on a wall nearby. Sits. Flies off.

He doesn't write a note.

He takes out his phone. Calls his father.

His father answers on the second ring, still half-asleep, the familiar voice:

*"Mone?"*

*"Appa,"* Neean says.

Just that. His father's voice and his own name and the city and the light.

He starts walking. Toward what, the film doesn't say. He's walking with the specific quality of someone who knows what they're walking through and has decided to walk through it anyway.

Tripti falls into step beside him. She arrived from wherever she was waiting. No explanation.

They walk.

The city runs around them. The simulation doing its best. The wrongness present and legible and enormous.

Neither of them looks at the crow.

---

**NEEAN**

*A film about what you see at 3am when the sum doesn't add up. And what you do the morning after.*

---

**Post-credits:**

A phone. A message. From a number with no name.

*The Source told you the truth about the cycle. It didn't tell you about me.*

*I've been in every layer.*

*— S*

The screen goes dark.
