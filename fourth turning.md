The Fourth Turning is a generational theory developed by William Strauss and Neil Howe that suggests American history (and to some extent Western history) moves in predictable 80-100 year cycles called "saecula," with each saeculum containing four ~20-25 year periods or "turnings."

Here are the four turnings and their characteristics:

1. First Turning (High): An era of growth and confidence following a crisis. Institutions are strong, individualism is weak, and society is confident about where it wants to go collectively.

2. Second Turning (Awakening): A passionate era of spiritual upheaval and cultural revolution, where the old civic order comes under attack. Think the Consciousness Revolution of the 1960s.

3. Third Turning (Unraveling): A period where institutions weaken and individualism strengthens. Society fragments and loses faith in conventional wisdom. Think the Culture Wars of the 1990s.

4. Fourth Turning (Crisis): A decisive era of upheaval when the old order is destroyed and replaced. Think the Great Depression and World War II period. These are times of maximum danger but also maximum opportunity for societal renewal.

Each turning also helps shape a particular generational archetype that repeats in the same order:

- Prophet (born in High)
- Nomad (born in Awakening)
- Hero (born in Unraveling)
- Artist (born in Crisis)

According to the theory, we are currently in a Fourth Turning that began around 2008 with the financial crisis. During Fourth Turnings, society faces its greatest challenges and undergoes its most profound transformations.

The key insight of the theory is that these cycles are predictable based on generational dynamics - as each generation ages into new life phases, they collectively shape the social mood and create the conditions for the next turning. The theory suggests that understanding these patterns can help society better prepare for and navigate through crisis periods.
