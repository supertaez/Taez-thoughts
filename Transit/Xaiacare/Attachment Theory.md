## Core Principles  
Developed by [[John Bowlby]] and [[Mary Ainsworth]], attachment theory posits that early caregiver-child bonds shape lifelong emotional and relational patterns.  

### Attachment Styles  
| Style           | Behavior with Strangers         | Long-Term Outcomes |  
|-----------------|----------------------------------|--------------------|  
| **Secure**      | Moderate [[Stranger Anxiety]]   | Healthy relationships |  
| **Avoidant**    | Minimal anxiety, aloof          | Emotional detachment |  
| **Ambivalent**  | High anxiety, clinginess         | Fear of abandonment |  
| **Disorganized**| Contradictory behaviors          | Psychopathology risk |  

---

## The Strange Situation  
Ainsworth’s lab procedure to classify attachment styles:  
1. **Separation**: Caregiver leaves infant with stranger.  
2. **Reunion**: Observes infant’s response to caregiver’s return.  

> [!TIP]- Clinical Use  
> The Strange Situation is criticized for cultural bias but remains a gold standard in [[Developmental Psychology Research]].  

---

## Modern Applications  
- **Parenting Programs**: Promote sensitive responsiveness ([[Circle of Security]]).  
- **Trauma Therapy**: Address disorganized attachment in [[Childhood Trauma]] cases.  

---

## Criticisms  
- **Cultural Limitations**: Overemphasizes independence (vs. collectivist norms; [[Cultural Childrearing Practices]]).  
- **Binary Classifications**: Fails to capture attachment fluidity (Granqvist et al., 2017).
