## Definition and Process  
**Social referencing** refers to a child’s tendency to look to caregivers for emotional cues when encountering novel stimuli (e.g., strangers, unfamiliar objects). This behavior emerges around **8–12 months** and is closely tied to [[Stranger Anxiety]].  

> [!EXAMPLE]- Classic Experiment  
> In the **Visual Cliff Experiment** (Gibson & Walk, 1960), infants relied on mothers’ facial expressions to decide whether to crawl over a perceived drop.  

---

## Mechanisms  
1. **Emotional Contagion**: Mirroring caregivers’ emotions (Hatfield et al., 1994).  
2. **Intentional Communication**: Infants actively seek cues via eye contact or vocalizations ([[Joint Attention]]).  

---

## Role in Development  
- **Risk Assessment**: Helps children evaluate safety (e.g., [[Stranger Anxiety]]).  
- **Cultural Transmission**: Teaches culturally specific norms ([[Cultural Childrearing Practices]]).  

---

## Individual Differences  
- **Secure Attachment**: Predicts accurate interpretation of cues ([[Attachment Theory]]).  
- **Autism Spectrum**: Reduced social referencing is a diagnostic marker (DSM-5).  

---

## Practical Applications  
- **Parent Coaching**: Encourage positive emotional modeling ([[Parenting Strategies]]).  
- **Early Intervention**: Used in therapies for developmental delays ([[Early Childhood Interventions]]).
